USE THIS RECORD FOR PRICING. It is customer master data entered by trained staff and has already
been confirmed against the photo, so it is reliable. It routinely carries detail a photograph
physically cannot show. Extract and apply whatever is present:

- **Physical dimensions** — e.g. "2700 x 800 x 750/1050 mm" = 2700mm long, 800mm deep, two-tier
  height (750mm work surface + 1050mm counter). For furniture, SIZE IS THE DOMINANT PRICE DRIVER:
  a 2700mm two-tier reception counter costs several times a 1200mm office table. Never price a
  large dimensioned unit at generic small-table rates.
- **Unit / seat count** — "6 seater", "set of 4", "3-seater sofa", "9U". Price the COMPLETE unit
  as described, not a single component.
- **Capacity and rating** — tonnage, litres, kVA, watts, sheets/min, BTU.
- **Brand, SKU, configuration** — CPU, RAM, storage, screen size, variant, generation.
- **Material and finish** — laminate, veneer, plywood, glass, MS/SS, leatherette, powder-coated.

Rules:
- If the record states a size, count, or configuration, your L–U MUST reflect it. Falling back to a
  generic class range while ignoring a stated specification is the single most common cause of a
  wrong valuation — do not do it.
- The photo shows WHICH asset it is; the record often states WHAT SPECIFICATION it is. Use both.
- Still ignore the declared cost — it must never influence your price estimate.
